## Line 4 — GIF Properties (Frame 4)

**Time:** 49.0 – 78.0s
**Delivery:** Explanatory; crisp contrast on the last two sentences.

    GIF Properties tracks rent, arrears and utilities. Two weekly automations split the work. The Updater records rent payments, rolls arrears forward, and refreshes utility readings and tenant status. The Verifier audits the workbook for missing or duplicate rent, balances that don't reconcile, stale readings and missing lease data, and flags what's wrong without touching the data. One keeps it current. The other keeps it trustworthy.

## Line 5 — GAK Properties (Frame 5)

**Time:** 78.0 – 109.0s
**Delivery:** Brisk through the list; deliberate on YES / CORRECT / OK.

    GAK Properties goes a step further. Every Friday, the Verifier reads all fifteen tabs of the workbook, plus tenant notices, utility bills, QuickBooks statements and bank alerts from a dedicated inbox, and emails a numbered list of findings: proposals, conflicts, and notices. A reply drives the Updater. Yes adds the figure. Correct writes the fixed value. OK just acknowledges. Every write is deduplicated, cross-checked, logged with evidence, and locked to GAK, so it can never touch GIF.

## Line 6 — Nour Hammam (Frame 6)

**Time:** 109.0 – 146.0s
**Delivery:** Steady; a small pause before 'needs ruling'. Three clipped words at the end.

    For Nour Hammam, a spa, a daily task acts as the registrar for the operations workbook. It reads six tabs: register, people, decisions, gates, money, and the change log. It only writes what a source supports, and every changed cell is logged with its old value, new value, and evidence. When something is missing or contradictory, it doesn't guess. It marks it: needs ruling. On the owner's dashboard, Refresh Data pulls all six tabs live, and re-maps every task to its current row, so edits land in the right place. Refresh. Review. Edit.

## Line 8 — Bills & Subscriptions (Frame 8)

**Time:** 159.0 – 197.0s
**Delivery:** Firm on 'nothing is estimated'.

    Bills and subscriptions span six entities: seventy-one recurring bills in one register. Every two weeks, an automation searches five mailboxes for invoices, renewals, payment confirmations and cancellations, and updates the register in place. It marks bills paid or cancelled, adds new ones, keeps the deadlines current, and stores evidence for every change. The rule is simple: nothing is estimated. If an amount can't be verified, it's marked unverified. The CEO dashboard turns it into four screens: the brief, decisions, dates, and every bill. And a separate task emails only the urgent items.
